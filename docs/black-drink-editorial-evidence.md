## pH, alkalinity and ionization: subsequent clarification

The client cannot supply a confirmed ingredient transcription and requests that the public site omit ingredient names. Respect that choice: keep the discussion general and do not label the drink as ionized, assign it a verified pH, or infer its mineral composition.

Primary definitions:

- IUPAC defines [pH](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/P04524) in terms of hydrogen-ion activity. Consumer paraphrase: a measure of how acidic or basic a liquid is.
- The USGS National Field Manual defines [alkalinity and acid neutralizing capacity](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/twri09A6.6) as measures of a sample's ability to neutralize strong acid. Alkalinity is not another name for pH. Its current [public explanation](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/alkalinity-and-water) also distinguishes the acid-neutralizing property from the pH reading.
- IUPAC defines [ionization](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/I03183) as generation of ions. This is a chemical process, not a definition of absorption, bioavailability or hydration performance.

Evidence boundary: these definitions cannot establish that a beverage is absorbed faster. A randomized, double-blind parallel trial of 100 healthy adults measured selected hydration markers after exercise-related dehydration. It found a difference in high-shear blood viscosity for the tested high-pH product but no significant treatment differences in plasma osmolality, bioimpedance or body mass. It did not measure intestinal absorption and did not test Zubrex. See [Weidman et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27932937/), DOI 10.1186/s12970-016-0153-8, [full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5126823/). Do not recast a surrogate marker as proof of faster absorption, and do not generalize a different formulation's results to this drink.

Suggested public note (about 100 words; no public reference links):

> Good product choices begin with precise language. pH describes how acidic or basic a liquid is; alkalinity describes its capacity to neutralise added acid. They are related, but they measure different things. Ionization means the formation of electrically charged particles, called ions. None of these terms, on its own, tells you how quickly a drink is absorbed or establishes a health benefit.
